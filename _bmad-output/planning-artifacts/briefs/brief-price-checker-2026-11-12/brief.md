# Product Brief: Price Checker

## Executive Summary

Price Checker is a mobile app for Android and iOS for shoppers in Romania. Scan a product's barcode, see its current price at evoMAG, Altex and PC Garage (each subject to the compliance gate), follow it, and get a push notification when the price drops or the item comes back in stock.

The app has two jobs, and the order matters. First, it is the worked example for presenting the BMAD method and a team of specialized AI agents: an internal team talk first, then a conference talk. Second, it must be a real, working product running against real retailers, because that realism is what makes the demo credible. **When a choice is between more features and a clearer demo, the clearer demo wins.**

The brief is therefore deliberately small in product scope and strict about two things a live audience will notice: whether the core journeys work on stage, and whether the product treats the retailers it reads from responsibly.

## The Problem

A shopper sees a product, in a store or online, and wants to pay the best price for it. Prices at the major Romanian online retailers change often, and the only way to compare them is by hand: open each retailer's site or app, search, compare, and repeat days later if the price was not right. In a store, with the product in hand, that is enough friction that most people either buy on the spot or walk away unsure. For anyone waiting for a better price, there is no single place to say "tell me when this gets cheaper, wherever it is."

## The Solution

One barcode scan replaces the manual search across retailers. The experience is five journeys:

- **Scan and compare (J1).** Scan the EAN barcode and see the product at each approved retailer: name, price in RON, availability and last-checked time. If the match is uncertain, the user confirms it. If nothing is found, the app offers the link fallback.
- **Paste a link (J2).** Paste or share a product link from a supported retailer to get the same view.
- **Follow and set alerts (J3).** Choose a target price, a percentage drop, or back in stock.
- **Receive an alert (J4).** A push notification opens the product with its current and previous price, the retailer, last-checked time and price history, plus a link to buy on the retailer's site.
- **Manage the watchlist (J5).** See all followed products, edit rules, unfollow, or delete all your data.

There are no accounts and no passwords. The app identifies users by an app-generated device ID. Each followed product is checked a few times a day, and checked only once per run no matter how many people follow it.

## Who This Serves

| User | Situation | What success looks like |
|---|---|---|
| In-store shopper | Standing in a store with the product in hand | Learns within seconds whether it is cheaper online right now |
| Patient buyer | Wants the product, but not at today's price | Gets one trustworthy notification when the price is right, and no false alarms |
| Presenter | Showing the app live on stage | Every core journey works on a real phone, every time, and tells a clear story |

The presenter is a first-class user, not an afterthought. Demo reliability is a product requirement here.

## What Makes This Different

Price Checker makes no claim to a market moat, and this brief does not invent one. The landscape scan (see `addendum.md`) found the following:

- **Romanian price tracking happens mostly in browser extensions**, such as Pricy, Istoric Preturi and xPrice. They offer price history and alerts but have no native app and no barcode scanning. Compari.ro advertises an app with barcode reading and alerts, but it is unclear whether that app is still live.
- **eMAG's own app already sends price-drop and back-in-stock alerts**, but only for its own catalog, and it has no barcode scanner.
- **The closest comparable abroad is Keepa**: scan a barcode in a store, then get alerts. It works for Amazon only.

So the honest position is **"Keepa for Romanian retailers"**: scan in the store and compare across retailers, which no retailer's own app does. On top of that:

- **"Price drop" is based on the app's own observed history**, not on the retailer's crossed-out price. This matches the spirit of Romania's 30-day prior-price rule (Omnibus Directive, HG 686/2022).
- **Responsible fetching is built into the product** (next section), not bolted on.

The real differentiator is the build itself: a product specified, built, tested and repaired by a team of AI agents under the BMAD method, with a visible artifact at every phase.

## Responsible Fetching

The product reads public retailer pages, and how it does that is part of what the talk shows. These rules are not optional:

- **Compliance gate.** A retailer is fetched only after it is marked Approved in `docs/source-register.md`. If a retailer is excluded, another Romanian retailer replaces it.
- **Minimal fetching.** Only public pages, fetched while logged out. Only followed products are checked, plus one lookup per new barcode, and there is no catalog crawling. Each product is fetched at most once per check. Per-retailer rate limits apply, the app honors Retry-After, and it backs off on errors.
- **Honesty.** The app sends an honest user agent with a contact URL. It never uses proxies, CAPTCHA solving or fingerprint spoofing. If a retailer blocks it, fetching stops and the block is flagged.
- **Facts only.** The app stores the price, currency, availability, seller, timestamp and URL. It never copies descriptions or images.
- **Kill switch.** Each retailer can be switched off without a deploy.
- **Accuracy over noise.** A missing price counts as a failure, never as "free." A large price change, such as more than 50%, is checked again before any alert goes out.

## Success Criteria

**Demo (the primary measure)**
1. Scan to alert: scan a real product, follow it, trigger a check and receive a push alert, all live.
2. Compliance gate: a retailer moves from Not checked to Approved before its adapter is allowed to run.
3. Self-healing adapter: a simulated layout change makes the tests and the health monitor fail. An agent proposes a fix, the tests pass, and a human approves it.
4. Every BMAD phase leaves a git tag and artifacts that can be shown on screen.

**Product integrity**
- No false alerts: no alert is ever sent for a missing price or for a large change that was not re-checked.
- Freshness: every followed product is checked a few times a day, and its last-checked time is always visible.
- Core journeys J1, J3 and J4 work on a real phone against the real approved retailers.

**Process quality (what the talk measures)**
- Fewer defects: defects caught in review and tests, compared with defects found after merge, per epic.
- Better docs: the share of stories that updated docs or registers, and no stale planning artifacts at the end of a phase.
- Predictability: time spent per BMAD phase and per story.

[ASSUMPTION: there are no adoption or usage targets, because the app is a demo vehicle.]

## Scope

**In the first release:** EAN scanning; mapping a barcode to product pages at approved retailers, cached for all users; the pasted or shared link fallback; the per-retailer price view; follow and unfollow; three kinds of alert rule; push notifications on Android and iOS; price history; deleting all data in the app; scheduled checks. The baseline also includes WCAG 2.2 AA basics, GDPR, all data in one EU Azure region, and prices in RON including VAT.

**Explicitly out:** payments or checkout; any monetization; retailers outside Romania; user-typed text search of retailer catalogs [ASSUMPTION: looking up a scanned EAN on a retailer's site is allowed, as fetching limit 3 permits]; a web app; layouts optimized for tablets; accounts with passwords or social login.

## Decisions Already Made

The stack is fixed: Flutter for the mobile app, Java 21 with Spring Boot for the backend, and a contract-first API (`api/openapi.yaml`) with generated server interfaces and Dart client. Data lives in Azure PostgreSQL Flexible Server. The API runs on Azure Container Apps, and price checks run as a scheduled Container Apps job, in a new dedicated subscription. Infrastructure is Terraform (plan on pull request, apply after approval), push goes through Firebase Cloud Messaging, and CI/CD is GitHub Actions with OIDC, all in one monorepo. Details are in `docs/initial-spec.md` §11.
